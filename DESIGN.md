# Verbatim — The Examiner's Bench

The world is a forensic document examiner's report. The questioned document lies on the bench
under raked light; what the eye sees and what the instrument sees are two registers of the same
page. Findings are numbered and each one is anchored to a locator in the document. **Nothing is
asserted without a plate reference.**

This is not decoration. It is the product's thesis made visible: every claim is a literal quote,
located in the source and verified before anyone reads it.

## Light

Light, always. Chosen from the use scene, not the category: an office manager at a desk in the
afternoon, reading documents for two hours. Paper is the correct ground for a long reading task,
and the world is a bench under a lamp.

## Palette

Bench neutrals with exactly one signal colour.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--paper` | `#faf8f4` | The bench surface |
| `--sheet` | `#ffffff` | A document on it |
| `--ink` | `#1a1815` | Text |
| `--graphite` | `#5c574f` | Secondary text, tinted from the ground, never gray |
| `--hairline` | `#ddd7cc` | Rules |
| `--steel` | `#2d4550` | Instrument chrome, the apparatus voice |
| `--amber` | `#b06a00` | **Reserved by law** |
| `--amber-wash` | `#fdf0d6` | The raking-light highlight on a located quote |
| `--oxide` | `#8f2f22` | **Reserved by law** |

**The two laws.** `--amber` marks *what the instrument found that the eye missed* — a located
quote, a concealed span. `--oxide` marks *a claim that could not be verified*. Neither appears
anywhere else, ever. Ninety per cent of candidates render with no signal colour at all, and that
is what makes the ten per cent legible.

Taken from the arcade challenger this direction beat: a reserved colour is a law, not an accent.

## Type

IBM Plex, self-hosted. One family designed as a system, with an engineered character that
belongs on an instrument bench — and not the grotesque every dashboard already uses.

- **Plex Serif** — the document itself. Résumé text, opening descriptions, anything the reader
  reads as prose. It is a document; it should be set like one.
- **Plex Sans** — the apparatus. Labels, controls, findings, navigation.
- **Plex Mono** — measurement only. Locators, offsets, scores, token counts, dates. Never as a
  costume for "technical".

Tabular figures everywhere a number can change. Body measure 65–75ch. Findings numbered in a
fixed margin column.

## Composition

Two registers, side by side: the ranked exhibits and the document under examination. Rules do
the separating, not cards. There are no cards on a bench — there are sheets, rules and margins.

**Fixed furniture.** Every candidate puts the same information in the same position, always, so
the eye learns where to look and stops searching. Taken from the character-catalogue challenger:
a finite ranked roster earns fixed personal furniture.

**Weight sets size.** A criterion worth 30 of the rubric's 100 is drawn larger than one worth 15.
The layout obeys the rubric's own structure rather than a uniform grid. Taken from the tensegrity
challenger: let the force network set the layout, not the page.

## The signature interaction: raking light

Claim ↔ locator, bidirectional.

Focusing a finding lifts its quote in the document: the span takes `--amber-wash` and its
character offset appears in the margin in Plex Mono. Focusing a highlighted span in the document
travels the other way, to the finding that cites it.

The set piece is the tampered résumé. The visible register is what a human reads; a toggle
raises the concealed layer, and the hidden text surfaces in `--amber` exactly where it sits on
the page — the oblique-light plate of an examiner's report.

Motion: one authored moment, the raise. Exponential ease-out, 180ms, from an already-visible
default. Nothing else animates.

## Two surfaces, one world

- **The panel** is Verbatim's, and carries the full apparatus.
- **The public application page** is the *hiring company's*. It inherits only the clean half:
  the company's name and colour, one column, generous measure, Plex Serif, no apparatus, no
  signal colours. The applicant is filling in a form, not standing at an instrument. Verbatim
  signs it once, quietly, at the foot.

## The honest risk

Forensic language can read as accusatory. If the panel feels like a fraud investigation, ordinary
candidates feel like suspects. The mitigation is the palette law: the apparatus stays neutral,
and the signal colours only appear where there is genuinely something to show.
